Toxic to dogs: Xylitol

Sugar substitute, also birch sugar

Found in

Trident Original Sugar Free Gum · Ice Breakers Ice Cubes Peppermint Gum · Tom's of Maine Whole Care Toothpaste, Spearmint · Epic Xylitol Peppermint Mints

Save image Save for TikTok

Xylitol, also known as birch sugar, is a sugar substitute used in some sugar-free gum, mints and toothpaste and in some peanut butters. In dogs it sets off a fast release of insulin that can drop blood sugar sharply, and Merck Veterinary Manual links doses above about 100 mg/kg to low blood sugar and reports that some dogs above 500 mg/kg may develop liver failure.

Signs reported in dogs

  • Vomiting
  • Weakness
  • Loss of coordination (ataxia)
  • Collapse
  • Seizures from low blood sugar (hypoglycemic seizures)
  • Liver damage (hepatic necrosis)
  • Bleeding problems (coagulopathy)
  • Yellow gums, skin or eyes (jaundice)

Low blood sugar can develop within 30 minutes or be delayed up to 12 to 18 hours.
